# SK Hynix

SK Hynix is the dominant leader in the HBM market and a key supplier of advanced memory for AI infrastructure.

## Strategic Shift: Memory Suppliers as AI Lab Shareholders (May 2026)

In a landmark restructuring of AI supply chain power dynamics, the three major HBM suppliers — [[sk-hynix]], [[samsung]], and [[micron]] — jointly invested in Anthropic as part of its $65 billion funding round (post-money valuation exceeding OpenAI). This marks the first time upstream memory suppliers have taken equity positions in downstream AI labs, signaling a strategic shift from pure supplier-customer relationships to vertically integrated partnerships. The move secures long-term HBM demand commitments and gives memory makers direct influence over AI hardware design roadmaps.

## Operational Risk Event: Cheongju Fire (June 1, 2026)

- **Incident:** Fire broke out in a fluorine gas handling room at SK Hynix's Cheongju 4th campus advanced memory plant
- **Impact:** 7 hospitalized, all 3,600 staff evacuated
- **Significance:** Cheongju is SK Hynix's primary HBM production facility. Fluorine gas is used in etching processes for advanced memory. Any disruption at this facility directly impacts HBM supply — already the tightest bottleneck in the AI supply chain.
- **Status (June 13 update):** SK Hynix confirmed fire initially controlled, **production equipment operating normally**. No confirmed supply disruption. Cheongju campus fire on June 12 was a separate incident at the same campus — also controlled with no production impact reported.
- **Assessment:** Two fire incidents at the same campus within 12 days raises operational risk concerns, but confirmed no production impact to date. Given the facility's critical role in HBM4 production for NVIDIA's Rubin platform, continued monitoring warranted.

## Wall Street Leverage Crackdown (June 2026)

- **Major banks (Citi, JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs) raising financing costs** for hedge funds using swaps to bet on SK Hynix and Samsung stocks
- Banks tightening new trade sizes, restricting counterparty access, and in some cases **refusing new swap requests** outright
- Similar measures applied to TSMC positions
- Trigger: sharp rally in Asian chip stocks YTD raising correction concerns
- Significance: Wall Street signaling that the memory chip trade is overcrowded — leveraged longs being squeezed out. Could create short-term selling pressure even as fundamental supply-demand remains tight.

## Rebellions Partnership — Memory-Centric AI Chips (June 2026)

- Korean AI chip startup [[rebellions]] leveraging SK Hynix (and Samsung) memory technologies for **memory-centric AI chip architecture**
- Rebellions designing AI inference chips that prioritize memory bandwidth over raw compute — a structural bet that memory, not GPU FLOPS, is the binding constraint
- Rebellions weighing IPO options, with SK Hynix/Samsung partnerships as key differentiator
- Signals Korean memory makers expanding downstream into AI chip ecosystem — not just supplying components but co-designing architectures

## Enterprise AI Adoption (June 2026)

- SK Hynix CEO 郭鲁正 evaluating adoption of **Microsoft 365 CoPilot and ChatGPT** for internal workflows
- Seeking balance between protecting industrial technology and expanding AI application
- Signals even memory manufacturers are accelerating internal AI adoption — the AI transformation extends across the entire supply chain

## Market Position

- **HBM market share:** 57% (Q3 2025, per Astute Group)
- Set to supply ~70% of HBM4 units for NVIDIA's next-generation Rubin platform
- Surpassed Samsung in annual profits for the first time in 2025 — a symbolic event showcasing the tectonic shift HBM has brought to the industry

## HBM Technology

- Began mass production of HBM4 in February 2026, 3–4 months ahead of schedule
- HBM4 operating speed: 11.7Gbps (46% above JEDEC 8Gbps standard)
- Adopted femtosecond laser technology for wafer thinning (20–30 micrometers)
- Unveiled "H3" hybrid architecture integrating HBM + HBF

## HBF Strategy

- Co-development with SanDisk (NAND expertise)
- Targeting sample shipments H2 2026, mass production early 2027
- Pursuing industry standardization approach (vs. Samsung's solo development)

## Market Outlook

- 2026 HBM market projected at $54.6 billion, growing 58% YoY
- Analysts project SK Hynix targeting quarterly operating profit of KRW 30 trillion
- DRAM market expected to remain in shortage throughout 2026
- **June 2026: SK Hynix announced plan to double memory wafer capacity within 5 years** — a direct response to structural HBM/DRAM shortage. This expansion will support HBM4 ramp for NVIDIA's Rubin platform and growing demand from AI hyperscalers. Signals management's conviction that memory shortage will persist through the decade.

## NVIDIA Co-Development & Supply Agreement (June 2026)

- NVIDIA and SK Hynix signed a **multi-year collaboration agreement** to co-develop next-generation memory technologies for NVIDIA's upcoming platforms
- SK Hynix will serve as the designated supplier for these co-developed memory products
- The agreement explicitly aims to address **extended development cycles** — as memory nodes advance (HBM4 → HBM4E → HBF), co-development ensures tighter alignment between GPU roadmap and memory specifications
- This formalizes the existing supply relationship and gives SK Hynix privileged access to NVIDIA's future platform requirements, potentially widening the gap vs. [[samsung]] and [[micron]]
- Jensen Huang publicly stated that SK Hynix's plan to double wafer capacity by 2030 is **"still not enough"** — underscoring the severity of the memory supply-demand imbalance
