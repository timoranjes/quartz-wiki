### Phase in July 2026

Geomagnetic storm frequency is reduced compared to 2024-2025 but remains significant enough to produce Kp 5-7 events several times per month.

## 2. Kp Index Thresholds for Tasmania (~43°S Latitude)

| Kp | NOAA Class | Tasmania Visibility |
|----|------------|---------------------|
| 0-2 | None | No aurora visible |
| 3-4 | None | Not typically visible in Tasmania |
| 5 | G1 Minor Storm | Aurora MAY be visible on southern horizon from darkest sites (Bortle 1-2). Camera detection likely before naked-eye. |
| 6 | G2 Moderate Storm | Aurora clearly visible from Tasmania with naked eye. May reach low on horizon in Victoria. |
| 7 | G3 Strong Storm | Aurora visible across Tasmania, often overhead. Visible from Victoria, SA, WA, far south NSW. |
| 8 | G4 Severe Storm | Major display across all of southern Australia. Visible from mid-NSW, southern QLD in rare cases. |
| 9 | G5 Extreme Storm | Historic event. Aurora may be visible across most of Australia. |

### Important Caveat
The Kp index has known limitations for southern hemisphere aurora forecasting. Of the 8 ground-based magnetometers used to compute Kp, **7 are in the northern hemisphere**. The Australian Space Weather Services (SWS) provides a southern-hemisphere-specific K-index that is often more reliable for Tasmania. A local K-index of 5+ at Tasmanian magnetometer stations (e.g., Hobart) is a better predictor of local aurora visibility than the global Kp alone. Additionally, the interplanetary magnetic field Bz component (southward/negative Bz is favourable) and solar wind speed are critical real-time factors.

**Sources:** Australian Space Weather Services (SWS), Shameless Visuals, theaurorazone.com

## 4. Dark Sky Quantification (Bortle Scale)

### Bortle Scale Reference

| Class | Title | NELM | SQM (mag/arcsec²) |
|-------|-------|------|-------------------|
| 1 | Excellent dark-sky site | 7.6-8.0 | ~21.9 |
| 2 | Typical truly dark site | 7.1-7.5 | ~21.7 |
| 3 | Rural sky | 6.6-7.0 | ~21.4 |
| 4 | Brighter rural / suburban transition | 6.3-6.5 | ~21.0 |
| 5 | Suburban sky | 5.6-6.5 | ~20.0-20.5 |

**NELM** = Naked Eye Limiting Magnitude (higher is darker)  
**SQM** = Sky Quality Meter reading (higher is darker)

### Bruny Island Classification

#### Southern Bruny (Cloudy Bay, Cape Bruny)
- **Bortle Class:** 1-2
- **Description:** Excellent dark sky. The southernmost parts of Bruny Island (Cloudy Bay, Cape Bruny, South Bruny National Park) are among the darkest inhabited locations in Australia. No towns, minimal development, ocean to the south.

#### Central Bruny (The Neck area)
- **Bortle Class:** 2-3
- **Description:** Very dark to truly dark. The Neck isthmus and surrounding areas have minimal light sources. Some distant light from Adventure Bay settlement visible but not impactful.

#### North Bruny (near Dennes Point/ferries)
- **Bortle Class:** 3-4
- **Description:** Rural to brighter rural. North Bruny has the island's main settlements (Alonnah, Dennes Point) and the ferry terminal. Still significantly darker than mainland Tasmania.

#### Overall Assessment
Most of Bruny Island falls in **Bortle Class 2-3**, placing it among the darkest accessible locations in Australia. Only remote wilderness areas in central Tasmania or the Southern Ocean islands are darker.

**Source:** lightpollutionmap.app (NOAA VIIRS 2025 data), Bortle scale (Wikipedia)

## 5. Best Locations on Bruny Island for Aurora Viewing

### [1] The Neck Lookout
- **Coordinates:** -43.317, 147.283
- **Bortle Class:** 2-3
- **Southern Horizon:** Unobstructed ocean view to south
- **Access:** Sealed road to carpark, short walk to lookout
- **Description:** Iconic narrow isthmus connecting North and South Bruny Island. Elevated position with 360-degree views. Unobstructed southern horizon over the Southern Ocean. One of the most-photographed aurora locations on the island.
- **Note:** Travel slowly at night — unsealed roads and wildlife (wallabies, pademelons)

### [2] Cloudy Bay
- **Coordinates:** -43.567, 147.217
- **Bortle Class:** 1-2
- **Southern Horizon:** Unobstructed — direct ocean to Antarctic
- **Access:** Unsealed road, 4WD recommended in wet conditions
- **Description:** Southernmost beach on Bruny Island. Completely unobstructed southern horizon over the Southern Ocean. Minimal light pollution. Famous for aurora photography — multiple properties here market themselves as 'home of the Aurora Australis'.
- **Note:** Remote, no facilities. Bring supplies. Check road conditions.

### [3] Cape Bruny Lighthouse
- **Coordinates:** -43.617, 147.150
- **Bortle Class:** 1-2
- **Southern Horizon:** Unobstructed — 180° ocean view
- **Access:** Unsealed road, rough track to lighthouse
- **Description:** Far southern tip of Bruny Island. Elevated lighthouse with dramatic Southern Ocean backdrop. Excellent for astrophotography with foreground interest. Among the southernmost accessible points in Australia.
- **Note:** Historic site. Very remote. No lighting for kilometres.

### [4] Adventure Bay
- **Coordinates:** -43.417, 147.333
- **Bortle Class:** 2-3
- **Southern Horizon:** Largely unobstructed from elevated positions
- **Access:** Sealed road, easy access
- **Description:** Eastern side of South Bruny. Sheltered bay with dark skies behind coastal bushland. Bruny Island Haven luxury retreats are perched above the bay with uninterrupted southern horizon views. Good balance of accessibility and darkness.
- **Note:** Small settlement but very low light pollution at night

### [5] Alonnah Pontoon
- **Coordinates:** -43.333, 147.250
- **Bortle Class:** 3-4
- **Southern Horizon:** Partially obstructed by terrain; better from water's edge
- **Access:** Sealed road, easy
- **Description:** Western side of North Bruny. Small settlement but dark skies looking south over Recherche Bay. Convenient if staying in North Bruny. Less dark than southern locations.
- **Note:** Closest 'dark' spot to the ferry terminal

### [6] South Bruny National Park (interior)
- **Coordinates:** -43.533, 147.200
- **Bortle Class:** 1
- **Southern Horizon:** Depends on clearing; coastal areas unobstructed
- **Access:** Bushwalking only, no vehicle access to interior
- **Description:** Large wilderness area covering the southern half of Bruny Island. The darkest skies on the island. No development, no light sources. Requires bushwalking access to clearings with southern horizon views.
- **Note:** Not suitable for casual viewing. For experienced bushwalkers.

## 7. What Makes a Property Good for Aurora Viewing

### [1] Dark sky rating (Importance: Critical)
Bortle Class 3 or darker is ideal; Bortle 1-2 is optimal. At Bortle 4+, only bright aurora (Kp 7+) is visible to naked eye. SQM reading of 21.4+ mag/arcsec² is the minimum for reliable Kp 5-6 aurora detection.

### [2] Unobstructed southern horizon (Importance: Critical)
Aurora australis appears in the southern sky. A clear view from due south (180° azimuth) to at least 30° elevation is needed. Coastal properties with ocean to the south are ideal because the auroral oval sits low on the horizon from Tasmania.

### [3] Elevation (Importance: High)
Higher positions reduce atmospheric interference, extend the visible horizon, and reduce the impact of ground-level haze. Even 50-100m elevation makes a measurable difference.

### [4] Distance from light pollution (Importance: Critical)
Away from towns, streetlights, bright buildings, and vehicle headlights. Even a single bright light source can wash out faint aurora. Properties should be >2 km from any settlement.

### [5] Minimal tree canopy to the south (Importance: High)
Tall trees block low-horizon aurora displays. Since the auroral oval sits at 10-30° elevation from Tasmania, even medium-height trees can obscure the display. Look for properties with cleared southern aspects or coastal exposure above the tree line.

### [6] Coastal exposure (Importance: High)
Ocean to the south means no terrestrial light sources on the horizon. Coastal locations also benefit from cleaner air and less atmospheric scattering. Bruny Island's southern coasts have the Southern Ocean all the way to Antarctica.

### [7] Weather patterns (Importance: Moderate)
Winter offers the clearest skies in Tasmania — less cloud cover, lower humidity, and more stable air. July is in the heart of Tasmania's aurora season (March-September, peaking June-July).

### [8] Moon phase (Importance: High)
New moon periods are 3-5x better than full moon for faint aurora detection. The July 2026 forecast coincides with the new moon (July 14), which is optimal. Plan viewing around moon-free nights.

### [9] Accessibility at night (Importance: Moderate)
Ability to access the viewing spot between 10 PM and 2 AM. On Bruny Island, unsealed roads and wildlife mean slow driving at night. Properties with on-site viewing (decks, clearings) are strongly preferred.

### [10] Accommodation features (Importance: Moderate)
Large windows or skylights facing south, decks with southern aspect, minimal external lighting on the property, ability to stay up late without disturbing others. Some properties offer "aurora alerts" or wake-up services.
