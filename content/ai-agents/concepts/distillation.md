
# Knowledge Distillation

Knowledge distillation is a model compression technique where a smaller "student" model is trained to mimic the behavior of a larger "teacher" model, achieving comparable capabilities at a fraction of the computational cost. In the current LLM landscape, distillation has become the primary pathway for creating capable small models and reasoning-specialized variants.

---

## Definition

Knowledge distillation transfers knowledge from a large, capable teacher model to a smaller student model through supervised fine-tuning on the teacher's outputs. Unlike traditional pre-training on raw text, distillation uses the teacher's generated responses (often with chain-of-thought reasoning) as training data, enabling the student to learn not just what to answer but how to reason.

## Distillation Pipeline

### 1. Data Generation

The teacher model generates high-quality responses on a diverse dataset:
- **Reasoning traces**: Step-by-step solutions for math, coding, and logic problems
- **Instruction following**: High-quality responses across many task types
- **Self-correction**: Teacher generates initial response, critiques it, and produces improved version

### 2. Filtering & Curation

Generated data is filtered for quality:
- **Automated filtering**: Remove low-confidence or self-contradictory outputs
- **Diversity sampling**: Ensure coverage across task types and difficulty levels
- **Deduplication**: Remove near-duplicate training examples

### 3. Student Training

The student model is fine-tuned on the curated dataset:
- **Supervised fine-tuning (SFT)**: Standard training on teacher-generated examples
- **Distillation loss**: May include KL divergence between teacher and student output distributions
- **Multi-stage**: Often multiple rounds of generation → filtering → training

## The R1 Distill Chain

DeepSeek's R1 distillation series has become the most influential distillation effort in the open-weight ecosystem:

### DeepSeek-R1-Distill Models

| Model | Teacher | Parameters | Key Benchmarks |
|-------|---------|-----------|----------------|
| **R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B** | DeepSeek-R1 | 1.5B | AIME 2024: 28.7%, MATH-500: 83.9% |
| **R1-Distill-Qwen-7B** | DeepSeek-R1 | 7B | AIME 2024: 55.5%, MATH-500: 92.8% |
| **R1-Distill-Qwen-14B** | DeepSeek-R1 | 14B | AIME 2024: 69.7%, MATH-500: 93.9% |
| **R1-Distill-Qwen-32B** | DeepSeek-R1 | 32B | AIME 2024: 72.6%, MATH-500: 94.3% |
| **R1-Distill-Llama-8B** | DeepSeek-R1 | 8B | AIME 2024: 50.4%, MATH-500: 89.1% |
| **R1-Distill-Llama-70B** | DeepSeek-R1 | 70B | AIME 2024: 70.0%, MATH-500: 94.5% |

**Key insight**: R1-distilled models punch dramatically above their weight class. The 7B distilled model outperforms many undistilled 70B models on reasoning benchmarks.

## Provider Approaches

### DeepSeek

DeepSeek pioneered the modern reasoning distillation approach:

- **DeepSeek-R1**: Teacher model trained with reinforcement learning on reasoning tasks
- **Open-weight distills**: Released distilled versions at 1.5B, 7B, 14B, 32B (Qwen base) and 8B, 70B (Llama base)
- **Apache 2.0 license**: All distilled models are fully open-weight
- **Impact**: Catalyzed an entire ecosystem of distilled reasoning models

### Microsoft Phi

Microsoft's Phi family uses a data-centric distillation approach:

- **"Textbooks Are All You Need"**: Phi models trained on high-quality synthetic data generated by larger models
- **Synthetic data generation**: Sophisticated pipelines for generating textbook-quality training examples with strict quality filters
- **Phi-4-reasoning** (14B): Outperforms DeepSeek-R1 distilled 70B model across reasoning tasks
- **Curriculum learning**: Training data ordered from simple to complex
- **MIT License**: All Phi models are open-weight under permissive licensing

### Alibaba Qwen

Alibaba's Qwen ecosystem both provides base models for distillation and performs its own distillation:

- **Qwen as distillation target**: R1-Distill-Qwen series uses Qwen models as the student base
- **Qwen3 Rerank**: Distilled reranking model for RAG pipelines
- **Model Studio**: Hosts both distilled models and provides tools for custom distillation

### Google Gemini / Gemma

Google's approach to distillation:

- **Gemma models**: Distilled from Gemini's capabilities into smaller open-weight models
- **Gemma 3**: Latest generation with improved reasoning through distillation
- **Synthetic data**: Google uses Gemini to generate training data for Gemma fine-tuning

### Anthropic Claude

Anthropic has discussed distillation in the context of its model development:

- **Claude Sonnet/Haiku**: Smaller Claude models benefit from knowledge transfer from Opus
- **Distillation API**: OpenAI's model distillation API (GPT-4o → GPT-4o-mini pattern) provides a similar approach for Claude users
- **Safety distillation**: Distilling safety behaviors from larger aligned models into smaller variants

### Meta Llama

Meta provides distillation tools and documentation:

- **Distillation guides**: Official documentation for training smaller models to match larger Llama models
- **Llama 3.3 70B**: Effectively a distilled/compressed version matching Llama 3.1 405B performance
- **Community distills**: Llama serves as the base for many community distillation efforts (including R1-Distill-Llama series)

### Together AI

Together AI supports distillation workflows:

- **Distillation API**: Offers model distillation as a service
- **Open-weight ecosystem**: Hosts and serves distilled models from multiple providers

## Co-distillation

Co-distillation (or codistillation) involves training multiple models simultaneously, with each model learning from the others' outputs:

- **Mutual knowledge transfer**: Models at different scales or with different architectures learn from each other
- **Diverse teacher ensemble**: Using multiple teachers can produce more robust student models
- **Research frontier**: Active area of research with potential for improved distillation quality

### Meta's Behemoth → Llama 4 Codistillation

Meta has employed large-scale codistillation in the development of Llama 4:

- **Behemoth teacher**: Meta's largest internal model (reportedly 10T+ parameters) serves as the primary teacher
- **Multi-scale students**: Llama 4 is trained at multiple sizes (8B, 70B, 400B+) with cross-scale knowledge transfer
- **Codistillation pipeline**: Smaller Llama models learn from larger ones, while larger models benefit from the diversity of smaller model outputs
- **Efficiency gain**: Codistillation reduces the compute needed for each model size by leveraging knowledge from siblings
- **Architectural diversity**: Llama 4's mixture-of-experts architecture benefits from codistillation across expert configurations

## Synthetic Data Generation

Synthetic data is central to modern distillation:

- **Teacher-generated examples**: Large models generate training data for smaller models
- **Quality filtering**: Only high-confidence, diverse examples are retained
- **Curriculum design**: Data is organized from simple to complex for effective learning
- **Data decontamination**: Rigorous removal of benchmark-adjacent data ensures genuine capability
- **Scale**: Distillation datasets can contain millions of teacher-generated examples

## Trade-offs

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Capability gap** | Distilled models match teacher on trained tasks but may lag on novel domains |
| **Reasoning transfer** | Chain-of-thought reasoning transfers well; creative generation less so |
| **Compute efficiency** | Training a distilled model costs ~1–10% of training from scratch |
| **Deployment cost** | Distilled models are 10–100× cheaper to run than teachers |
| **Licensing** | Distilled models inherit licensing from both base model and training data |

## Best Practices

1. **Match architecture**: Students perform best when their architecture is similar to the teacher
2. **Quality over quantity**: 100K high-quality distilled examples often outperform 1M noisy ones
3. **Multi-task coverage**: Include diverse task types to avoid overfitting to a single domain
4. **Evaluate on held-out tasks**: Test distilled models on tasks not in the distillation dataset
5. **Iterative refinement**: Multiple rounds of generation → evaluation → retraining improve quality
